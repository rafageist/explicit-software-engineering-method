# Glossary

\begin{multicols}{2}

**Artifact**
A produced, versioned, and inspectable work product that affects system behavior and can be validated and traced to intent or decisions.

**Assumption**
A statement treated as true for reasoning or planning when evidence is missing or deferred, and that must remain identifiable until validated or invalidated.

**Compensation**
An explicit and auditable acceptance of risk due to a missing required artifact or validation, recorded without removing the underlying obligation.

**Contextual Parameters**
Declared inputs that characterize the engineering context and shape the required level of explicitness and validation for a given scope.

**Decision**
A committed engineering choice among alternatives, made explicit and linked to the intent it serves.

**Diagnosability**
The ability to explain a failure in method terms using explicit evidence of intents, decisions, assumptions, validations, compensations, and parameters.

**Explicit**
Engineering reasoning captured as traceable artifacts with stated intent and validation rather than remaining undocumented or implicit.

**Explicitness**
The property of engineering work being made explicit in a form that is traceable, evaluable, and diagnosable.

**Failure**
An observed deviation from intended or required behavior or constraints that can be analyzed against explicit engineering reasoning.

**Intent**
The desired outcome, property, or constraint that a change or system must satisfy, with a defined scope.

**Method**
A prescriptive and executable set of obligations and constraints over engineering artifacts and relationships, parameterizable and analyzable, and independent of workflow order.

**Method Application**
The concrete application of the method to a specific scope with declared parameters and explicit artifacts such that compliance and diagnosability can be evaluated.

**Normative Rule**
A mandatory statement of obligation that defines what must be explicit and the consequences of omission.

**Orthogonality**
Independence from workflow order or lifecycle sequence while maintaining fixed engineering obligations.

**Process**
An ordered sequence of activities or phases used to organize work, which may embed a method but does not define engineering obligations by itself.

**Software**
Executable code, configurations, and data that produce system behavior.

**Software Product**
Software whose behavior, constraints, and purpose are accountable through explicit intent, decisions, validation, and traceability.

**Traceability**
Explicit and navigable links among intents, decisions, assumptions, artifacts, and validations that allow reasoning to be inspected and evaluated.

**Validation**
Evidence that an artifact or decision satisfies its stated intent under the declared context.

\end{multicols}
