# 1. Why This Is a Method

The Explicit Software Engineering Method is defined as a method, not a methodology or a process. This is a technical distinction that affects how the work is executed and evaluated.

## 1.1 Methodology vs Method vs Process
- Methodology: the study and justification of methods; explanatory and conceptual.
- Method: a prescriptive, executable way of working defined by explicit artifacts, rules, and constraints.
- Process: an ordered sequence of activities or phases; a workflow that can embed a method.

## 1.2 Why "Method" Fits
The method defines explicit artifacts and relationships that can be instantiated, checked, and analyzed.
It is parameterizable, so teams can adapt explicitness levels while keeping traceability and validation requirements.

## 1.3 Why Not a Process
The method does not enforce a fixed sequence of steps.
Instead, it defines compensations and constraints that keep engineering work balanced even when workflows vary.

## 1.4 Implication
Because the method is explicit and executable, it can be enforced by tooling and assessed with evidence rather than anecdote.
